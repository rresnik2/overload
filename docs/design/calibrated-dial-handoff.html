Calibrated Dial

This spec covers the chosen A+B direction: the Calibrated look (rubber-floor dark, plate colours, condensed italic type) with a plate-face rotary dial for weight entry, and a picture of the equipment that reacts as the dial turns.

Source: direction mockup, section A+B Stack: Next 16, React 19, Tailwind 4 + daisyUI 5 Status: ready for build · 7 decisions recorded 15 Oct Checked against: main at 20dfb63 (after the UX merge)

01Overview

Lifters log a set between efforts, often with chalky or sweaty hands, standing next to the equipment. Today the weight field is a text input between −/+ buttons that move one step per tap. Going from 82.5 to 100 kg takes seven taps.

The new mobile set editor replaces that with three things:

  • A big readout of the weight and reps, readable at arm’s length. Tap it to type an exact number.
  • A plate-face dial. One click of the dial is one step for that equipment (2.5 kg on a barbell, 2 kg on dumbbells, the next bell size on kettlebells, one pin on a machine stack).
  • An equipment picture above the dial that shows what the number means: plates per side on the bar, the size of the dumbbells or bell, or how many plates the pin lifts.

Reps keep a horizontal number strip and RIR/RPE keeps five buttons, as in direction A. The rest of the app adopts A’s palette and type through new daisyUI themes.

Why a dial and not a slider: a slider’s precision depends on its length, so on a 320 px screen with 120 values each value gets under 3 px. A dial gives every value the same 12° of travel however many values there are, and you can keep turning it.

04Layout

Mobile active-set panel (< 768 px)

The first incomplete set of the focused exercise renders as ActiveSetPanel. The other sets of that exercise render as compact SetRows above and below it. Tapping a compact row makes it the active set. The panel is one column at the page gutter. Heights below are at 320 pt content width (an iPhone mini). Everything scales with width except the fixed heights.

  • Order and focus order: top bar, name, meta, set legend (the existing SetLegend hint buttons, one line, about 24 px), picture, caption, readout, dial, reps, RIR/RPE, Log set. The legend stays visible on phones: e2e/ux.spec.ts checks for it.
  • Log set is position: sticky; bottom: calc(18px + env(safe-area-inset-bottom)), so it stays reachable when the panel is taller than the screen.
  • Short screens (max-height: 700px, e.g. iPhone SE) use --picture-h: 88px and a 160 px dial. The panel scrolls and Log set stays pinned.
  • Rest bar: the existing .wk-rest bar sits above Log set while a rest is running, adding --wk-rest-h to the bottom offset.
  • No equipment picture (bodyweight-plus, plates, medicine ball, other): the picture and caption rows collapse, and the dial moves up.
  • No weight field (setFields().weight === false): there is no readout weight and no dial. Reps and RIR only.

Why one set at a time: on a phone the set you are about to do is the only one you edit. Giving it the full width makes room for the dial and the picture. The finished sets above it are a record, not inputs.

Why typing only on desktop: with a keyboard, typing “102.5” is quicker than turning anything, and dragging a dial with a mouse is awkward. Base the choice on (pointer: coarse) as well as width, so an iPad in landscape still gets the dial.

Swipe navigation (phones)

Three horizontal gestures, all by touch or pen pointer events (useSwipe, components/shell). A mouse never swipes, and every gesture has a button or link that does the same thing.

GestureWhereWhat it does
Swipe left / right/workout (dial layout)Moves the dial panel to the next / previous exercise in workout order, with a short slide from that side. No wrap-around. The visible path is the ‹ Prev · 2 of 5 · Next › bar; its buttons name the exercise they go to and are disabled at the ends.
Swipe left / right/today, /exercises, /progress only (under 768 px)Goes to the next / previous tab in dock order with nav-forward / nav-back. No wrap-around. Nested pages and the More pages ignore it. A dock tap picks the same direction from the tab’s position.
Edge swipeInstalled iPhone/iPad app only (display-mode: standalone on iOS)A touch within 20 px of the left edge drags the page with the finger; releasing past 64 px (or with a fling) goes back one history entry as nav-back, otherwise it snaps back. Does nothing without an earlier entry. Android and Safari already have a system back gesture, so it is off there.
  • Intent: horizontal only. The move must pass 10 px, then |dx| > 1.5 × |dy|; it commits at 64 px or a fling (at least 24 px at 0.5 px/ms). Vertical intent cancels for good and the page scrolls as normal; the hook never calls preventDefault. Swipe areas use touch-action: pan-y pinch-zoom.
  • Opt-outs: a gesture that starts inside [data-no-swipe], a form field, or an element that really scrolls sideways is ignored. data-no-swipe is on the Dial, NumberStrip, Tabs and ModalityTabs, .exercise-tabs, .settings-nav, chart frames (plots and table scrollers), the learn table, the PR tables, dialogs and sheets, and the Prev/Next bar.
  • Reduced motion: the gestures still work; the slides and the snap-back have no animation.
  • Page slides are about 200 ms in total (--page-out 80 ms, --page-in 120 ms), and ::view-transition has pointer-events: none so a tap during a slide reaches the page.

Bar profiles and plate inventory (D1, D6)

  • Bar weight per exercise. A bar profile is a MachineProfile linked by exerciseId. Its baseLoadKg is the bar weight. Catalog exercises are global and read-only, so the override lives on this per-user profile, not on the exercise. With no profile, the defaults are barbell 20 kg / 45 lb, EZ bar 10 kg / 25 lb and trap bar 25 kg / 55 lb.
  • Plates owned. The new nullable platesKg on the profile lists the plate weights the gym has, for example [25, 20, 15, 10, 5, 2.5, 1.25, 0.5]. Null means the standard set for the unit. Loading uses only those plates. Dial values are every total the inventory can make, from the bar weight up to the range cap, so a gym without 1.25 kg plates steps by 5 kg. Plates not in the table (0.5 kg, 1 lb) draw as chrome with width 4 and height 26.
  • Editing. A “Bar and plates” link under the barbell caption opens the profile sheet. The same profile is also listed in Settings → Machines. The sheet has a bar weight field (a NumberStepper) and a checkbox chip for each plate weight.
  • Smith and plate-loaded machines use the same profile: baseLoadKg is the sled or carriage weight, and platesKg drives the loading.

Gear profiles: dumbbell step and owned kettlebells

  • What it is. A gear profile is a MachineProfile with the new nullable equipment set to DUMBBELL or KETTLEBELL, exerciseId null and gymName null. It is set once per equipment and applies to every dumbbell or kettlebell exercise. Machine lists (the machine chooser, the template editor, Settings → Machines) show only profiles with equipment null.
  • Dumbbells: pinStepKg is the step the user sets (default 2 kg / 5 lb). The dial runs from one step to 80 kg / 180 lb in that step.
  • Kettlebells: platesKg is the sorted list of bells the user owns (at most 20, each up to 100 kg). Null means the standard sizes above. The dial offers exactly those bells. A value from history that is not among them is still inserted in order.
  • Editing. A link under the picture caption reads “Dumbbells · 2 kg steps · Edit” or “Kettlebells · your 6 bells · Edit” and opens the same kind of sheet as “Bar and plates”; the first save creates the profile. Settings → Machines and gear lists both under a Gear section.
  • Per gym later. The same row shape with gymName set will override the user-wide profile for that gym. pickGear(rows, equipment, gymName?) and repo.machineProfiles.gear(equipment, gymName?) already take the optional gym and fall back to the user-wide profile, so no schema or lookup redesign is needed.

Barbell and plate-loaded

  • Canvas: viewBox 300 × 92, centred in the picture row. Sleeves are 0–92 and 208–300 (height 10, --steel). The shaft is 96–204 (height 4, --steel-dark). Collars are 7 × 22 at x 90 and 203.
  • Plates (height × width, viewBox units): 25/55 = 86 × 13, 20/45 = 86 × 11, 15/35 = 74 × 10, 10/25 = 60 × 9, 5/10 = 44 × 7, 2.5/5 = 36 × 6, 1.25/2.5 = 30 × 5. Corner radius 2, gap 1.5. They stack outward from the collar, heaviest innermost. The right side starts at x 211; the left mirrors it from x 89.
  • Loading is greedy, heaviest first, from (total − bar) / 2. If the remainder isn’t 0, exact = false: draw the nearest loadable weight below, and the caption says “101 kg can’t be loaded exactly · nearest 100 kg”. The entered value is still saved as typed.
  • Overflow: if a side’s plates are wider than 85 units, scale plate widths down (to 60% at least), then draw the rest as a “+n” badge.
  • Plate-loaded machines draw the right horn only (x 120–300) and load perSide directly.

Dumbbell

  • Canvas: viewBox 288 × 120. Two dumbbells centred at x 72 and 216, or one at 144. Handle 48 × 8 (--steel). The hex heads are drawn at full size, 34 × 92 with chamfer 9, fill #2B2E33 and stroke #4D535A.
  • Scale: heads scale with k = (26 + 66 · √((w − 2) / 58)) / 92, clamped between 0.28 and 1. The left head’s transform origin is its right edge, and the right head’s is its left edge (transform-box: fill-box). The square root keeps light weights visible and stops heavy ones from overflowing.

Kettlebell

  • Canvas: viewBox 288 × 120. The bell is drawn at 48 kg size and scaled from its base centre with k = 0.42 + 0.58 · √((w − 4) / 44). The handle is a 12-unit stroke in #2B2E33, and the stamped number is 24-unit Archivo condensed italic.
  • Values (kg): 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48. Values (lb) are the kg sizes converted and rounded the way bells are labelled: 9, 13, 18, 22, 26, 31, 35, 44, 53, 62, 70, 80, 88, 97, 106. Each lb size takes the colour of its kg size. A value from history that isn’t on the list (for example 18 kg) is inserted in order.
  • Colour by size: 4 #C7C9CC · 6 #F2B7CC · 8 #EC6FA3 · 10 #8CC6EA · 12 #2F6FD8 · 14 #8D9399 · 16 #EFBD2A · 20 #8A5CC9 · 24 #2F9A4F · 28 #EF7C22 · 32 #D8262D · 36 #5F6369 · 40 #ECEBE6 · 44 #B9BDC2 · 48 #C9A646. Weight stack

  • Canvas: viewBox 288 × 120. Guide rods at x 76 and 212. Twenty plates, each 124 × 4 at a 5.2 pitch, starting at y 14. Selected plates fill with --color-base-content and move translateY(-10); the others fill #33373C. The pin is --color-primary. The pin label sits at x 236 and “STACK n × step” at x 8.
  • Pin number: pinFor(value, step, base) = round((value − base) / step) + 1 when baseLoadKg is set, else round(value / step).
  • Stack size is unknown (it’s not in MachineProfile). Draw a window of 20 plates. If the pin goes past plate 17, the window scrolls so the pin stays 3 from the bottom.

07The dial

  • Turning: pointer events with setPointerCapture. Track the angle from the centre, normalise each change into (−180°, 180°], and add it up. Each time the total passes ±detentDeg, step the index by ±1. Clockwise increases.
  • Rotation shown = index × detentDeg, set on the rotating group as an SVG transform. It snaps per detent with no tween while dragging, which reads as a click.
  • Limits: at the first or last value, the extra turn is thrown away (the total resets). The dial plays the limit bump (section 09).
  • Touch: touch-action: none on the dial only. A drag that starts on the dial never scrolls the page.
  • Mouse wheel: only when the dial has focus (after a click). Every 30 px of deltaY is one detent. It never captures page scroll while hovering.
  • Rim colour: on a barbell it’s the tone of the heaviest plate on the bar (steel when the bar is empty), with ink from the plate table. Everything else uses --color-primary with white ink.
  • Value null (a new set with no ghost): the dial points at restIndex (the bar weight, 10 kg dumbbells, 16 kg bell, pin 1). The readout shows “—”. The first detent sets the value.

10Content rules

  • Exercise name: up to 2 lines with line-clamp: 2, then an ellipsis. The 2-line form drops to 30 px. The full name is always in the accessible name. Longest catalog names are around 40 characters (e.g. “Single-arm cable lateral raise”).
  • Meta line: “Barbell · 20 kg bar · set 3 of 4 · last 100 × 5 @ RIR 2”. It may wrap to 2 lines. Drop “last …” first if it would need a third.
  • Numbers: up to 2 decimals, with trailing zeros dropped (formatWeight). The readout drops to 52 px when the integer part has 4+ digits.
  • Units: “KG”, “LB”, “KG / HAND”, “KG / SIDE”, taken from unitLabel() and loadLabel().short, uppercased in the label style. lb rows use the lb plate set and lb steps.
  • Effort: labels come from effortOptions(effortScale): 0 1 2 3 4+ for RIR, or 10 9 8 7 6 for RPE. Warm-up sets hide the effort row (existing rule).
  • Copy: “Log set”, “Logged ✓”, “Per side”, “Pair of 32 kg · 64 kg total”, “Pin 9 of 20”, “Empty bar · 20 kg”, “101 kg can’t be loaded exactly · nearest 100 kg”, “Set a weight or reps first”. The app is English only today, but keep these strings together in labels.ts so they can be translated later.

12Accessibility

  • Use a native range as the source of truth. Put a visually hidden <input type="range" min="0" max={values.length − 1} step="1"> inside Dial, with aria-valuetext from valueText, and mark the SVG aria-hidden. VoiceOver’s swipe up/down and TalkBack’s volume keys then adjust it natively, which ARIA role="slider" on a div doesn’t reliably get on iOS.
  • Value text: “102.5 kilograms total”, “32 kilograms per hand”, “Pin 9, 45 kilograms”. Build it from loadLabel().help wording.
  • Keyboard: Arrow up/right is +1 step, Arrow down/left is −1, Page Up/Down is ±4 steps, and Home/End go to the ends. Enter on the readout opens typing, and Esc cancels.
  • Focus order: readout, dial, reps strip, effort buttons, Log set. Focus is never trapped in the panel.
  • Picture: role="img" with a label (“Bar loaded with 25, 15 and 1.25 kilograms per side”). Not live, because the slider already announces the value.
  • Announcements: after Log set, a polite live region says “Set 3 logged, 102.5 kilograms, 5 reps” (the existing toast region can carry it).
  • Targets: the dial is at least 160 px; reps items are 46 × 50; effort buttons and Finish are 44 px tall.
  • Contrast: see the token tables. Plate blue is never used for small text on dark, and success green with white text is used only at large or bold sizes.
  • Reduced motion: section 09. The dial keeps working, with no tweens.
  • Tap-to-type is the fallback for anyone who can’t do circular gestures, and it is always available.

13Haptics (dropped after a device test)

The feature was built as specified in the first draft (an in-repo tick() / confirm() helper using the Vibration API on Android and the <input type="checkbox" switch> technique on iOS, with a Settings toggle). It was dropped after a device test: no haptics fired on an iPhone or on an Android phone. The helper (haptics.ts and its test), the Haptics toggle in Settings → Profile and every call in ActiveSetPanel were deleted.

What stays: Dial.onDetent remains as a generic callback (nothing uses it now), and the rest timer keeps its own separate vibration in hooks.ts. A stale overload:haptics-off key in localStorage is harmless and is never read.

14Decisions

All seven open questions were settled with the product owner on 15 October 2026.

  1. D1 · Bar weights: per-exercise override. Stored on a bar profile (a MachineProfile with exerciseId and baseLoadKg), with defaults per bar type when there’s no profile. No schema change for this part (section 06).
  2. D2 · Light theme: build from this spec. overload-daylight ships with the values in section 03 and is reviewed in the app. No separate mockup.
  3. D3 · Desktop: typing only. Fine pointers at ≥ 1024 px keep NumberStepper typing with the picture as a preview, and show no dial.

  6. D6 · Plate inventory: included now. A new nullable platesKg on MachineProfile (an additive change request). Loading and dial values follow it.
